Thonet No. 14

Michael Thonet, 1859

History

Michael Thonet was a cabinetmaker from Boppard, in Germany, who spent years working out how to steam beech wood and bend it into curves. He moved to Vienna in 1842, and in 1859 his firm began selling the No. 14, a café chair built almost entirely from bent rods of beech.

The chair has six pieces of wood, ten screws and two nuts. It could be shipped in parts, with dozens of chairs packed into a single crate, and put together at its destination. That made it cheap enough for cafés and ordinary homes, and it was sold as the consumer chair, Konsumstuhl in German.

An estimated 50 million had been sold by 1930. Le Corbusier admired Thonet's bentwood chairs and used them in his own interiors, and a version of the No. 14 is still made today.
