Windsor chair

English chairmakers, early 1700s

History

The Windsor chair first appeared in England in the early 1700s. Chairmakers in the Thames Valley and around High Wycombe turned the legs, spindles and bow on a lathe, then fitted them into a solid seat shaped from a single plank. There was no frame, so a Windsor was light, strong and cheap to make.

The name has no certain origin. One common explanation is that the chairs were sold through the market town of Windsor in Berkshire. By the 1730s they were being made in Philadelphia, and the form spread through the American colonies, where makers used whatever local wood suited each part.

The chair in the photograph is a fan-backed Windsor made in Philadelphia between 1760 and 1770, from tulip poplar, maple, hickory and oak. Using a different wood for each part was typical: a soft wood for the carved seat, and tough, springy wood for the spindles and legs.
