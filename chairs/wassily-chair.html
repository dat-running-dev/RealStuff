Wassily Chair

Marcel Breuer, 1925

History

Marcel Breuer designed this club chair in 1925 at the Bauhaus in Dessau, where he ran the furniture workshop. He had bought a new Adler bicycle and wondered whether furniture could be made by bending steel tube the way the handlebars were bent.

The result was one of the first chairs made from tubular steel. Instead of padding on a heavy frame, it uses slings of fabric or leather stretched between the tubes. The structure is visible, it can be taken apart, and the whole chair is light.

It is known as the Wassily Chair because Wassily Kandinsky, a teacher at the Bauhaus, owned an early copy. The name was added later, in the 1960s, when the Italian firm Gavina put the chair back into production. Knoll took over production afterwards.
