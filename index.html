Chair Guide

Nine chairs, and where they came from.

A short history of chairs people still sit in, from an eighteenth-century country chair to a plastic one from the 1960s.

Hover over a chair for a preview. Select it for the full story.Touch and hold a chair for a preview. Tap it for the full story.

  • Windsor chair

    English chairmakers, early 1700s

    A spindle back, a solid wooden seat, and legs fitted straight into it.

  • Thonet No. 14

    Michael Thonet, 1859

    Six pieces of bent beech, ten screws and two nuts, shipped flat-packed.

  • Red and Blue Chair

    Gerrit Rietveld, 1918

    Thirteen lengths of timber and two boards, painted to show how they join.

  • Wassily Chair

    Marcel Breuer, 1925

    Bent steel tube, inspired by bicycle handlebars, with a sling seat and back.

  • Barcelona Chair

    Mies van der Rohe and Lilly Reich, 1929

    A steel X-frame and leather cushions, made for a pavilion that stood for less than a year.

  • Eames Lounge Chair

    Charles and Ray Eames, 1956

    Moulded plywood shells and leather cushions, inspired by a well-worn baseball glove.

  • Tulip Chair

    Eero Saarinen, 1957

    One pedestal instead of four legs, to clear the clutter under a table.

  • Egg Chair

    Arne Jacobsen, 1958

    Made for a hotel lobby, to give guests a quiet corner in a busy room.

  • Panton Chair

    Verner Panton, 1960

    A single curve of moulded plastic with no back legs.
